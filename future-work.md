# Future work — the census remainder

Every falsifiable thesis of the Idol program that is not in the
first tranche stands here, named, with its evidence state and
its closing condition. A thesis leaves this file only by landing
in this repository as a native proof, or by gaining a family
home strong enough to cite. Nothing here is counted as proved,
and no entry is silently dropped: an entry that is refuted keeps
its refutation as its state.

States use the four forms of the program: settled intent,
proposed semantics, implemented behavior, verified behavior —
with proven, disproven, open, and premise where the Lean record
earns them. Sources: the falsification register of 2026-11-17
(items F1 to F13 and the whole-construction items), the
decidability-engine spec (its section 24 and its neighboring
sections), the monotonicity record, the zero-loss ledger, and
the release record of 2026-11-18.

## From the spec, section 24 and its neighbors

### Census 18 — the composed theorem as one object

Thesis: the four parts of section 24.2 — finiteness,

### Census 19 — the linear cost shape of the coloring closure

Thesis: the coloring closure can be computed in work linear
in the sum of the rules, the facts of the universe, and the
conclusions the closure produces, by an indexed implementation
that charges each rule for the facts it consumes and each
conclusion once. State: proposed; the proof on disk bounds
the number of steps by the universe size and gives no linear
bound on total work, and no measurement on disk measures
closure cost against the three counts. Closing condition: an
indexed implementation built to that shape and measured on a
named snapshot whose counted work stays within the linear
sum, or a proof that no such indexing exists for the rule
form, which would move the state to disproven.

### Census 20 — the five-part completeness receipt

Thesis: a completeness claim for a coloring stands only on a
receipt in five separately bound parts — enumeration of
candidates, saturation of equalities, coverage of evidence,
closure of qualification, calculation of the frontier — each
with its own verdict, its own boundary where partial, and its
own evidence named. State: proposed as one receipt; its parts
stand in mixed states on disk and no artifact issues the five
parts as one receipt, and none computes the four coloring
states from ground rules in rule form. Closing condition: the
first artifact that issues this receipt as one object over a
named snapshot, its parts checked one by one against the
derivations they cite. The rule-form coloring joined to the
cited closure is part of this closing work: the rule form is
stated in Lean over the spec's snapshot shape and its
equivalence is proved against the cited closure, or a
counterexample separates them.

### Census 23 — the native witness effect

Thesis: executing a binding in the derivation machinery

### Census 24 — witness minimality

Thesis: a witness is a derivation-step node identity plus a

### Census 25 — implication closure as a derived object

Thesis: entailment edges between gates are derived and

### Census 26 — regression as a per-step derived predicate

Thesis: a step regresses exactly when its post-step frontier

required axis, or a gate in the proved state becomes
unresolved or disproved without a recorded premise change —
evaluated over the step's changed set only, with a
recomputable certificate per non-regressing step. State:
proposed as a per-step predicate; its bounds-level relative
(census 7) stands proven and executed-verified. Closing
condition: the predicate evaluated over the standing step
chain at a named pin, including the constructed negative
control step, with no disagreement against the checker's
independent evaluation.

### Census 27 — the scheduling generalization

## The zero-loss ledger claims, census 28 to 32

### Census 28 — the zero-loss law

Thesis: every benchmark ever measured or runnable where Idol
loses, ties short of the bar, or is absent is an open row in
the zero-loss ledger until a same-contract re-measure at a
current pin wins, and a benchmark in the records with no row
is a ledger defect. State: proposed — a standing directive
and record whose completeness over all records has never been
independently audited end to end. Closing condition: that
audit, at a named pin, exhibiting no benchmark without a row
and no row closed without its winning re-measure or its
retiring ruling.

### Census 29 — beat by construction

Thesis: a beat claim counts only when the win falls out of
the one derivation machinery, never from hand-tuning, a
hand-written kernel, or copying an incumbent's surface.
State: premise — a definitional premise of the whole ledger
program, carried by every dominance claim that rests on
ledger cells. It is never promoted; a claim counted as
satisfied whose producing mechanism is a manual optimization
is its refutation, and the first measurement under any such
claim must state its producing mechanism beforehand.

### Census 30 — always win, as a description of the present

Thesis: Idol currently wins every contested benchmark, by the
widest margin, with no loss standing. State: disproven — the
ledger itself is the refutation, with measured losses, ties
short of the bar, absences, and unmeasured cells printed at
their pins. As a directive (no loss is a resting state) the
sentence stands as settled intent; as a description it stays
here at the state its evidence earns, and it leaves this file
only when the ledger's own count reaches zero open loss rows
at a named pin.

### Census 31 — demand propagation as a performance claim

Thesis: severing demand propagation improves time or code
size decisively on a corpus with dead work. State: disproven
— at its recorded pin the severed-to-control ratios sat
inside the noise floor on every corpus and the emitted
artifact was byte-identical with prune severed on every loop
shape measured. The mechanism stays implemented as a
structure and correctness device. Closing condition for the
claim, not the mechanism: a same-contract re-measure where
severing moves time or size decisively, at a named pin.

### Census 32 — the Wasm faster-than-native bar

Thesis: the Wasm route runs the contested workloads faster
than any native arm under the same contract. State: disproven
as a present claim — on the two standing crypto specimens the
Wasm route scored slower than native on both shapes and the
native arm was absent on both. The bar stands as a directive
and an open ledger row. Closing condition: a same-contract
re-measure at a current pin on a qualified venue where the
Wasm route wins both shapes, or the bar is restated at the
strength the measurements survive.

## The monotonicity theses, census 34 to 44

### Census 34 — the certificate claim

Thesis: a release transition is certifiably monotonic exactly
when a version 3 certificate over a named projection carries
a monotonicity statement whose three legs each hold, fail by
a name, or print uncovered, with every element recomputable
from named artifacts by a checker that trusts no producer.
State: proposed; no certificate has issued at any rung, and
one executed instance stands at oracle level (a staging gate
run that held two legs on evidence, found no evidence for the
third, and returned not certified). Closing condition: the
first certificate issued at a named pin whose statement a
checker verifies from the named artifacts alone.

### Census 35 — carried verdicts over the anti-unification diff

Thesis: the state diff is the classification read off the
anti-unification of the two states, a candidate's premise
cone is exactly the holes it varies, and a verdict whose
premise cone does not intersect the holes holds of the
candidate unchanged — at completion exact, and under the
scope rules its spec states. State: proposed semantics; no
producer computes a diff in this form. Closing condition: a
producer at a named pin computing the diff in this form, with
every carried verdict in its battery surviving full
re-derivation over its candidate.

### Census 36 — velocity and acceleration from certificates

Thesis: semantic velocity and acceleration are derivable
from the certificate series alone, recomputable by any reader
of the series, with no self-reported numerator or denominator.
State: proposed; the definitions are true by construction
wherever the recorded quantities exist, and the certified
series is empty in every project, so every quantity stands
unmeasured and its five stated tests have never run. Closing
condition: the first certified series of length two or more
in one project, with a second reader reproducing every figure
from the series alone.

### Census 37 — the baseline is the latest known good

Thesis: every candidate has exactly one baseline — the
latest known good among the live states it replaces,
identified by a read of the frontier and never by an act of
choosing, freezing, or awaiting. State: premise — a settled
definitional premise of the monotonicity program, carried by
every gate comparison in census 34. It is never promoted; one
candidate whose parentage yields two distinct latest known
goods under the read, or none despite a checked record on its
line, is its refutation.

### Census 38 — zero-knowledge verdicts

Thesis: gate verdicts can be proven over committed graphs
without opening the content, with premise-set inclusion proven
per fact against the premise-set digest, transparent-setup
proof systems only, and a leakage budget named on the
certificate, bounded and disclosed and never zero. State:
proposed; settled intent and proposed semantics, not
implemented and not verified — today's gate recomputes from
full records and opens the content. Closing condition: one
verdict under this scheme that a checker verifies without
opening the content, at a named pin, with its premise set
matching its digest when opened afterward.

### Census 39 — the decidability boundary

Thesis: the gate is three-valued by design — every chosen
obligation resolves as proved, disproved, or unresolved, with
unresolved first-class and never silently converted — because
no total classifier over behavioral obligations exists, by
diagonalization. State: proposed in Lean terms; settled as a
boundary statement in its spec, resting on cited settled
reasoning rather than a new proof. Closing condition: none
stands against the boundary itself; finite-state obligations
and bounded fragments widen coverage without touching it, and
a total classifier that survives its own diagonal case is
its only refutation.

### Census 40 — the scoped elimination of routine human review

Thesis: within a defined scope, an automated gate that
derives obligations from the graph, independently checks
proofs, replays counterexamples, and runs reproducible
comparisons can approve changes whose evidence checks out,
reject demonstrated violations, and hold unresolved cases, so
no human sits in the normal merge loop. State: proposed;
settled intent, with the transport from the graph to the
checker and the qualified release gate not live. Closing
condition: the gate live at a named pin over its defined
scope, with no in-scope approval whose required evidence
fails recomputation and no demonstrated violation approved.

### Census 41 — the ratchet

Thesis: adding a checked fact to a derivation can only merge
holes in the anti-unification of two states, never
manufacture a genuine difference. State: proposed; an open
point of the monotonicity spec, shared with census 35.
Closing condition: machine-checked where the proof face
reaches, or the residue argued in the record and named; a
standing battery case is the control meanwhile.

### Census 42 — continuity over the ideal completion

Thesis: the derivation map preserves directed suprema over
the ideal completion of the history poset — deriving over a
prefix and passing to the limit agrees with deriving over the
whole history. State: proposed; over finite recorded
histories the statement adds nothing beyond monotonicity, and
its general form has neither a proof nor a counterexample on
record. Closing condition: a proof at a named pin, or one
named counterexample figure whose whole-history value differs
from the limit of its prefix values.

### Census 43 — the cost-fall prediction

Thesis: cone-scoped certification makes cost per
certification fall as carried verdicts accumulate, and that
fall is the mechanical source of certified acceleration.
State: proposed; no window has opened, because certified
releases stand at zero, so the provision stands armed and
untriggered and every cost figure is unmeasured. Closing
condition: the cost series over the first full enforced-era
window — the first eight full weeks from a project's first
certified release — showing the predicted fall; failing that,
the prediction drops by its own rule to a per-project
observation.

### Census 44 — semantic mass as a counting measure

Thesis: semantic mass is counting measure over the mined
equivalence classes — never negative, zero on the empty set,
additive over disjoint unions — so a transition's touched
symbols partition disjointly into added, removed, changed,
and formed classes whose masses sum exactly to the whole,
reconciled per point. State: executed-verified at sidecar
level over the recorded histories, with per-point
reconciliation; no gate consumes these figures, and mass has
no certified value while the certified series is empty.
Closing condition: a Lean form at a named pin, or the first
gate consuming a mass figure whose displayed value fails
additivity over its disjoint partition, which would move the
state to disproven.

## The monotonicity foundation claim, remainder (census 33)

Thesis: all software is a finite state machine over a
join-semilattice of equivalent terms. State: settled intent
as a directive, posed by its author as a question; the
readings of it are interpretation, and the developed answer
is proposed semantics. The algebraic half is proved natively
in this repository, in the merge file. Two parts remain. The
identification premise: that the states of a given piece of
software, under the equivalence the machinery checks, are
faithfully represented by the classes of the merge file.
Closing condition: the representation is constructed for one
named subject at a named pin and the faithfulness is proved,
or a state is exhibited that the representation cannot carry.
The state-machine half: that the nodes and transitions of a
subject map to the configurations of that semilattice.
Closing condition: the map is constructed for the same named
subject and its preservation of transitions is proved, or one
transition is exhibited with no image. The census flag stands
beside this entry: the thesis is not yet falsifiable in its
universal form until its order is named, and the algebraic
half proved here names the order for its representation only.

foreign-intermediary census at zero in the shipped path, and

## The PPC expectation (census 47)

Thesis: among semantically equivalent forms, the least-chars
form yields the most performance, power, and composability —
stated as a falsifiable, measured expectation, verified per
application at the same contract on every axis, never assumed.
State: proposed as a general claim, with named adverse
evidence on the speed axis: the library ports win compactness
by roughly four to five times and lose speed by factors from
single digits to thousands on a shared missing machinery face,
each loss entered as a machinery defect rather than a veto on
the shorter spelling. It is an empirical thesis, and no Lean
